# canon-sites

The places in `canon/places.yml` that can be located, as points. Built by `scripts/build-sites.js` from `data/sites-sources.json`, which records, for every site, how it was found.

- **Chapters and years.** Each site carries `chapters` (the canon entry's `appears`) and `years` (the years of its `by_year` entries). A map made for a chapter (`project.chapter`) shows the sites that appear in that chapter; with no chapter it shows those whose years include the map year. "any year" on the layer shows all.
- **Precision.** `point`: the building or spot (square marker). `area`: a district, park, campus or cemetery, at its centre (open ring). `town`: only the town is located (dotted ring). `vicinity`: the place is known only roughly, drawn as a dashed ring as wide as the doubt (`radius_m`), with a `confidence` of `stated` (the canon or a source names the spot, but not the building), `inferred` (placed from a street, a district or a neighbouring site) or `guess` (the canon gives a bound and little else). `fictional` marks a place the canon invents; the map labels it "(invented)". The ring says where the canon puts the place, not where a building stood.
- **Methods.** An OpenStreetMap object found through Nominatim and checked by eye (`data/sources/` raw results are in `sources/raw/sites/`, not committed); an intersection of two streets in the 1928 layer; a midpoint of two intersections; a town in `ga-places`; a feature of the lakes layer.
- **Licence.** OpenStreetMap: ODbL, credit "© OpenStreetMap contributors". Census Gazetteer: public domain.

## Known problems

- **113 of 117 canon sites are located**: 68 precisely (above), 45 as `vicinity` rings (added 2026-10-28). The vicinity rings come from four kinds of evidence: streets and corners the canon names (Houston Street at Boulevard, Wheat Street Baptist at Auburn and Fort, 5 East Alabama Street, Walnut and Ella); the neighbourhood centres of the *Black neighborhoods* layer (Brownsville, Shermantown, Buttermilk Bottom, Vine City, Summerhill); offsets from a located neighbour (the prison laundry and boarding house from the penitentiary, the 1917 storehouse between Decatur Street and Auburn Avenue, Beacon Hill from Decatur square); and bounds the canon gives and nothing more (the Roswell Road churchyard and ferry from Marietta square, Candler Field's Hammock and Seep, Belt Line bottoms). The `guess` rings are the least reliable; each entry's `basis` says what it rests on. Four are deliberately unplaced: the river burials (an invented placement the HALS report contradicts), the 1917 burn zone (an area, to be traced as an outline), the Chattahoochee (already a waterway line) and Grant's earthworks (a ring of forts, not a point). Earlier text, for the 68:; nine are traced from Sanborn sheets (the Ponce de Leon ballpark, the National Pencil Company, the Fulton Bag mill, the Union Depot, the Fulton County courthouse, Marietta's square, the Decatur jail and the DeKalb courthouse and the Atlanta Medical College), eleven more (added 2026-10-28) come from corners and addresses the canon states and a published source or two: the Terminal Station, Union Station (1930), Loew's Grand, the Municipal Auditorium, Jacobs' Pharmacy, the Fourth National Bank, Morris Brown College, the Atlanta Avenue sewer cut, Techwood Homes, University Homes, the City Stockade and the Klan's Imperial Palace; and then, from the HALS report, English's house on Cone Street (40 Cone Street) and the brickyard. For most of those the point is a street crossing or a midpoint (precision `area`), not a building, and each entry's `note` says so. The rest are listed by `npm run build-sites` as unlocated: invented sites (which must stay unplaced), sites whose period location the modern map doesn't show (Candler Field's 1941 west edge, the Fort McPherson war prison barracks, Camp Gordon's grounds, Rehoboth Baptist Church, Carrie Steele's cottage, Butler and Slater's drugstore) and streets. Trace them from the period maps with the backdrop layer, then add them to `sites-sources.json` with `canon` as the method and the sheet as the basis.
- **Present-day locations are not always period locations.** Friendship Baptist stood at Mitchell and Haynes in 1877 (the canon's own statement), which is about 450 m from the present church; the layer uses the canon's corner. Ebenezer Baptist's OpenStreetMap node is the newer church on Jackson Street, about 150 m from the 1877 address. Morris Brown's present campus is not its 1880s site, so it is not located.
- **Rejected first hits** (wrong object): "Georgia State" (a MARTA station, not the Capitol), The Masquerade (now in Underground Atlanta, not its North Avenue mill), a fast-food restaurant for University Homes, the Atlanta Municipal Court for the Fulton County courthouse, a church in Zion Hill 15 km west of Vine City.
- Town-level sites (`town` precision) are the town's Census centre, which can be a few hundred metres from its square.

## Leads not yet placed

- **The Chattahoochee Brick Company yard** is placed at the centre the National Park Service's Historic American Landscapes Survey report (HALS GA-7) states, 33.809103, -84.490475. The report's Figure 2, a scaled 1907–1961 site plan, is a georeferenced backdrop (`hals-ga-7-brick-site-plan`): it shows the 1907 buildings, tracks and convict-lease residences, and its own boundary centre falls 31 m from the stated centre. The only 1907 source for the layout is the 1907 Sanborn map, which the report cites but the project doesn't hold.
- **The Stockade** is placed at 750 Glenwood Avenue SE, today's building. The City's page gives 750 and the NPS 760 (about 100 m apart); neither is the stockade's own plan.
- **Streets and neighbourhoods** (Peachtree, Marietta, Decatur, Houston and Auburn streets, Wheat Street, Brownsville, Shermantown) are lines or districts: Auburn Avenue, Shermantown and Brownsville are in the *Black neighborhoods* layer.
